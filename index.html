Flames, fuels and the code that predicts them

Every project closes one loop: an experiment, the chemistry behind it, and a model that can predict the next one. Kinetic mechanisms are generated with RMG and tested in Cantera. Machine learning and physics-informed neural networks turn flame images and simulations into models that respect conservation laws. Techno-economic models ask whether a fuel is worth building at scale. Wherever possible the code is open source, so anyone can check it, reuse it and make it better.

    Research agents, coming soon

    Two research agents for Claude by Ahmet Alper Yontar, both working on open data only. They will be in service soon.

    Agent Simit

    Coming soon

    A literature and data agent for hydrogen combustion.

    Describe your study and give three keywords. Agent Simit searches OpenAlex, ranks the papers for your study, checks ChemKED and retractions, extracts ignition-delay and burning-velocity data from papers you can access, and turns verified data into open datasets. It also ranks kinetic mechanisms against the verified data in Cantera. Every value traces to a DOI and stays unverified until a person checks it.

    GTeA · Green Techno-economic Analysis

    Coming soon

    Techno-economic analysis of biofuels and biomass, on open data only.

    Ask what a biofuel or biomass pathway costs, emits or saves: its CO₂ abatement cost, break-even or parity price, price paths to 2035 and 2050, and its energy, water, land and indirect land-use footprint. Covers FAME biodiesel, maize and corn-stover ethanol, HVO, gasification, fast pyrolysis, wood pellets and biomethane.

      Enjoy: Symposium Run

      A pixel platform game for combustion students, with a climate mission. Travel through all 42 International Symposia on Combustion, from Swampscott 1928 to Kyoto 2026 and Brazil 2028, across eight eras that each play differently: thermochemistry, kinetics, laminar flames, droplets and sprays, turbulence, pollutants, detonation and the energy transition.

      • Two heroes, Spark the flame kernel and Droplet the fuel droplet, against Carbon, CO, Soot, NOx and CO₂
      • Each symposium city drawn as itself, with landmarks only from that year: palms in California, the Golden Gate in San Francisco, pagodas and torii in Kyoto, a castle in Edinburgh
      • Every era has its own mechanic: build the H₂–O₂ chain, outrun a flame at its burning velocity, survive the d²-law, ride turbulent eddies, defeat the NOx boss, escape a detonation wave
      • Classic combustion books introduced along the way, from Lewis and von Elbe to Law, Williams, Peters and Lee
      • Timed cloud questions with a global-warming thermometer as the clock, and a username-only leaderboard
      • Undergraduate and graduate modes: a full journey through all 42 symposia asks 200 questions, drawn fresh each run from a bank of 420
      • Every answer comes with a short explanation; numbers checked with Cantera
